Short-Form Content vs. Attention Metrics Dashboard

Introduction

We often hear that social media is destroying our attention spans. With short-form content becoming a daily habit, it seems reasonable to assume that more screen time leads to less focus.

We analyzed thousands of users to test that assumption. What we found challenged a popular belief and pointed to a different factor that may have a much greater impact on attention and productivity.

Screen Time May Not Be Responsible For Focus Levels

We started with the most common assumption: more screen time should lead to lower focus.

The charts tell a different story.

Instead, the relationship was surprisingly unclear. Focus levels varied across all screen-time ranges, and the correlation coefficient (r = 0.063) was nearly zero. With no strong pattern emerging, the data left us with a new question.

If screen time isn't strongly linked to focus, what is?

Note*: The value r in the chart on the right indicates correlation. Negative values closer to -1 indicate negative correlation. Positive values closer to 1 indicate positive correlation. Values close to 0 indicate no correlation.

The Real Suspect

If screen time is not driving focus levels, what is?

First, we examined whether the platform itself mattered. TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts showed very similar attention and focus patterns.

Sleep told a different story. As sleep increased, focus, attention span, and overall performance improved. The data suggests sleep may be a far stronger predictor of focus than either platform choice or screen time.

Further Exploration

We took one final look at productivity to see if screen time explained performance.

It didn't. Highly productive users appeared across nearly every level of screen usage.

The pattern reinforces what we've seen throughout the analysis: screen time alone is not enough to explain attention and productivity. Other factors, especially sleep, appear to play a much larger role.

Conclusion

The strongest paths in the Focus Journey Map begin with sleep. More sleep is associated with better focus and lower stress, while lower stress is linked to stronger focus and higher task completion rates. Together, these relationships suggest that productivity is shaped less by screen time alone and more by a connected chain of factors, with sleep playing a foundational role.

"The biggest plot twist wasn't what people were watching—it was how much they were sleeping."
